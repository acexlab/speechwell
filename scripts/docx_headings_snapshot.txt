0188	Heading1	INTRODUCTION
0189	Heading2	BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION
0190	Heading2	Human communication relies heavily on clear and structured speech. Speech production is a complex process involving coordination between respiratory, phonatory, articulatory, and neurological systems. Any disruption in these systems can lead to speech disorders that affect intelligibility, fluency, articulation, or language structure. Common speech disorders include dysarthria, stuttering, and phonological disorders, while language-level impairments may include grammatical and syntactic errors. These conditions significantly impact an individual’s academic performance, professional communication, and social interactions.
0191	Heading2	Dysarthria is a motor speech disorder caused by neurological impairment that affects muscle control, leading to slurred or slow speech. Stuttering is a fluency disorder characterized by repetitions, prolongations, and involuntary pauses. Phonological errors involve incorrect sound production patterns, while grammatical errors affect sentence structure and linguistic correctness. These disorders may occur independently or coexist, making assessment complex and multidimensional.
0192	Heading2	Traditionally, speech and language disorders are diagnosed and monitored by speechlanguage pathologists (SLPs) through clinical observation, structured assessments, and perceptual analysis. While effective, these methods require trained professionals, repeated sessions, and significant time investment. In many regions, access to speech therapy services is limited due to cost, geographic barriers or shortage of specialists. This creates a gap between the need for early diagnosis and the availability of professional evaluation.
0193	Heading2	Advancements in digital signal processing and artificial intelligence have opened new possibilities for automated speech assessment. Acoustic analysis techniques can capture measurable speech parameters such as energy, pitch, pause duration, and speaking rate. Additionally, speechtotext technologies enable linguistic evaluation, including grammar and phonological error detection. Machine learning models can learn patterns from these features and assist in identifying abnormal speech characteristics with high consistency.
0194	Heading2	Despite these advancements, many existing automated systems focus primarily on either acoustic features or linguistic errors, but rarely integrate both in a unified framework. Systems targeting fluency disorders may ignore grammar correction, while grammar correction tools often do not account for speechbased acoustic abnormalities. This separation limits the ability to perform comprehensive speech and language disorder screening.
0195	Heading2	SpeechWell is developed to address this gap by integrating acoustic feature extraction, linguistic analysis, machine learning classification, and personalized therapy recommendations into a single structured framework.
0196	Heading2	The primary motivation behind SpeechWell arises from the growing need for accessible, scalable, and automated speech assessment tools. Early detection of speech disorders is critical, especially in children and adolescents, as delayed intervention may lead to longterm communication challenges. However, many individuals do not receive timely assessment due to limited access to specialists or lack of awareness.
0197	Heading2	Another key motivation is the need for objective and quantifiable evaluation. Traditional clinical assessments often rely on subjective perceptual judgment. While expert evaluation is invaluable, incorporating measurable acoustic parameters such as speaking rate, pause frequency, energy variation, and pitch consistency can enhance reliability and reduce variability in assessment outcomes. Automated systems provide consistent and repeatable analysis across sessions.
0198	Heading2	SpeechWell is also motivated by the need to combine both motorlevel and languagelevel analysis. Speech disorders are not limited to fluency or articulation; they may also involve phonological and grammatical deviations. By integrating speechtotext transcription and grammar evaluation with acoustic feature analysis, the system provides a more holistic assessment of communication ability rather than focusing on a single dimension.
0199	Heading2	The use of machine learning, particularly logistic regression in the proposed system, is motivated by the requirement for interpretable and probabilitybased predictions. Logistic regression generates confidence scores that indicate the likelihood of a speech disorder, making the output understandable and clinically explainable. Instead of acting as a blackbox model, it provides structured decision boundaries based on extracted features, allowing thresholdbased severity classification (e.g., mild, moderate, severe).
0200	Heading2	Another major motivation is continuous monitoring and progress tracking. Speech therapy requires repeated practice and longterm engagement. Many individuals struggle to maintain structured practice sessions outside clinical settings. By storing sessionwise results and comparing performance over time, SpeechWell enables measurable progress tracking, which encourages user engagement and supports datadriven therapy adjustments.
0201	Heading2	Furthermore, therapy recommendation systems are often disconnected from assessment tools. After detecting a disorder, users must independently search for suitable exercises. SpeechWell addresses this issue by incorporating a structured videomapping logic. Based on predicted disorder type and severity level, the system recommends appropriate therapy exercises such as articulation drills, breathing exercises, fluency shaping techniques, or grammar correction practice. This ensures that assessment results are directly linked to actionable intervention steps.
0202	Heading2	From a technological perspective, the motivation also lies in demonstrating the effectiveness of integrating digital signal processing, natural language processing, and machine learning within a single architecture. The project showcases how acoustic features (MFCCs, pitch, energy, speech rate) can be combined with linguistic indicators (grammar errors, phonological deviations) to improve classification performance and interpretability.
0203	Heading2	Socially, the project is motivated by the increasing demand for AIassisted healthcare and educational tools. An automated, scalable system can support preliminary screening in schools, telehealth environments, and remote areas where professional resources are scarce.
0204	Heading2	Additionally, the project aims to contribute to the development of accessible speech analysis technologies that can assist individuals in monitoring and improving their speech over time. By providing objective analysis, progress tracking, and feedback through a digital platform, the system encourages early awareness of speech difficulties and promotes continuous improvement through guided exercises and training resources. This approach helps bridge the gap between professional therapy sessions and everyday practice, making speech support tools more accessible and practical for a wider population. Furthermore, the system demonstrates how modern web technologies and machine learning frameworks can be integrated to build an end-to-end intelligent application for real-world use. Such a platform not only supports automated analysis but also enables scalable deployment, user interaction and continuous improvement through data-driven insights.
0205	Heading2	Finally, SpeechWell is motivated by the need for an interpretable and modular architecture. By combining rulebased heuristics with a machine learning classifier, the system ensures explainability in its predictions. The inclusion of confidence scores, severity thresholds, grammar correction feedback and personalized therapy videos makes the platform comprehensive and usercentric.

0220	Heading3	Disfluency Assessment Using Deep Super Learners [1]
0221	Heading3	Speech disfluency, characterized by pauses, repetitions, and prolongations, is a key indicator of disorders such as stuttering and Specific Language Impairment (SLI). To automate fluency assessment, the Pipelined Deep Learner Dual Classifier (PDLDC) system was developed using speech signal processing and deep learning techniques. The framework extracts glottal and acoustic features such as open quotient, harmonic richness factor, and amplitude quotient from bilingual speech datasets, including the UCLASS dataset, to represent physiological and fluency characteristics.
0222	Heading3	The system integrates a Convolutional Autoencoder (CAE) for feature representation and a Super Learner Ensemble combining Random Forest, kNearest Neighbors, Logistic Regression, and XGBoost for classification. Using crossvalidation and evaluation metrics such as F1score and Cohen’s Kappa, the model achieved high accuracy and robustness. This hybrid deep learning architecture enables efficient, scalable, and datadriven speech disorder detection suitable for realtime speech therapy applications
0231	Heading3	Automatic Detection of Speech Sound Disorder in Cantonese-Speaking Pre School Children [2]
0232	Heading3	Speech Sound Disorder (SSD) is a developmental condition that affects a child’s ability to produce speech sounds accurately, reducing intelligibility and impacting academic and social development. To address limitations of manual assessment, an automated detection framework was developed for Cantonese-speaking preschool children using acoustic signal processing and machine learning techniques. The system captures speech recordings under controlled conditions and applies preprocessing steps such as noise reduction and normalization. It then extracts acoustic and phonetic features including pitch, formant frequencies, energy, spectral features, and tonal variations to account for Cantonese’s tonal structure.
0233	Heading3	The extracted features are used to train machine learning models such as Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest, and Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) for classifying typical and atypical speech production. Cross-validation techniques ensure model reliability and generalization across different speakers. Additionally, it provides detailed feedback by identifying specific problematic sound segments, supporting personalized therapy planning, progress tracking, and scalable early screening in clinical and educational settings
0242	Heading3	Automatic Disfluency Detection from Untranscribed Speech [3]
0243	Heading3	Speech disfluencies such as repetitions, pauses, and revisions interrupt the natural flow of speech and are important in clinical and speech technology applications. This framework introduces automatic frame-level disfluency detection using language-based, acoustic-based, and multimodal approaches. It utilizes ASR systems with BERT for text-based detection, and self-supervised speech models like Wav2Vec2.0, HuBERT, and WavLM for direct audio-based analysis, evaluated on the Switchboard Corpus.
0244	Heading3	To improve accuracy, a multimodal fusion model using a Bidirectional LSTM (BLSTM) combines acoustic and linguistic features. The system achieves strong performance, particularly with WavLM-based acoustic models, and works effectively even in noisy conditions. By leveraging deep learning and self-supervised technologies, the framework provides a scalable and efficient solution for real-time speech disorder detection and clinical applications.
0253	Heading3	StutterNet – Stuttering Disfluencies Detection in Synthetic Speech Signals via MFCC and Deep Learning [4]
0254	Heading3	Speech disfluency, especially stuttering, affects normal communication and can lead to social and emotional challenges. To automate detection, the proposed StutterNet framework uses the UCLASS stuttering dataset and applies preprocessing steps such as noise removal, duration normalization, SMOTE-based data balancing, and Voice Activity Detection (VAD). Each speech sample is converted into 40 Mel Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs), which capture important spectral features of human speech. These features are used to train and compare multiple machine learning models including Random Forest, SVM, MLP, AdaBoost, LSTM, RNN, and BiLSTM.
0255	Heading3	Among all models, the custom hybrid StutterNet architecture, built on MFCC feature extraction and a BiLSTM-based deep learning network, achieved the best performance with 92.85% accuracy using tenfold cross-validation. The system demonstrated stable training, strong precision and recall, and effective temporal analysis of speech patterns. With its lightweight eight-layer design and real-time capability, StutterNet supports automated stuttering detection, clinical monitoring, and integration into mobile or web-based speech therapy and voice-enabled applications.
0264	Heading3	A Text-Dependent End-to-End Speech Sound Disorder Detection and Diagnosis in Mandarin-Speaking Children [5]
0265	Heading3	Speech Sound Disorder (SSD) affects children’s ability to pronounce sounds correctly, impacting communication and learning. This paper proposes a text-dependent end-to-end deep learning model for automated SSD detection in Mandarin-speaking children. The system uses a specially collected Mandarin speech corpus from 100 children and applies acoustic and linguistic encoders combined with an attention-based decoder. By incorporating the Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) loss function, the model automatically aligns speech with phoneme sequences, eliminating manual segmentation and enabling efficient mispronunciation detection.
0266	Heading3	The architecture integrates acoustic feature extraction, linguistic modeling, attention mechanisms, and CTC-based alignment to identify phoneme-level errors and phonological processes. The proposed model outperformed baseline CNN–RNN CTC and MDD-EDA systems, achieving over 91% phone recognition accuracy. Its ability to detect substitution, deletion, and insertion errors makes it suitable for clinical diagnosis, personalized therapy planning, and scalable speech assessment applications.
0275	Heading3	Advancing Stuttering Detection via Data Augmentation, Class-Balanced Loss and Multi-Contextual Deep Learning [6]
0276	Heading3	Stuttering is difficult to detect automatically due to speaker variability and data imbalance. To address this, the improved framework applies deep learning techniques using acoustic feature extraction and multi-class classification to identify disfluencies such as repetitions, prolongations, blocks, and interjections. The model incorporates class-balanced learning with a weighted loss function, a multi-branch architecture to separate fluent and disfluent speech, and multi-context temporal processing to capture both short and long-duration stuttering patterns. Additionally, attention mechanisms are employed to focus the model on salient disfluency regions, further improving detection performance. Such automated stuttering analysis systems can assist speech-language pathologists by enabling objective assessment, continuous monitoring, and early intervention support.
0277	Heading3	To enhance robustness, data augmentation methods such as background noise addition and reverberation are used, improving generalization to real-world conditions. The system was evaluated on datasets including SEP-28k, FluencyBank, and LibriStutter, demonstrating strong accuracy, recall, and F1-scores across stuttering categories. By combining deep neural networks, class balancing, and multi-context feature modeling, the framework provides a scalable and clinically valuable solution for automated stuttering detection. The proposed approach also reduces manual annotation effort, making large-scale and real-time stuttering analysis more feasible in clinical and educational settings.
0286	Heading3	The Detection of Dysarthria Severity Levels Using AI Models [7]
0287	Heading3	Dysarthria is a motor speech disorder caused by neurological impairments that affect articulation, phonation, resonance, and prosody. Traditional severity assessment relies on perceptual evaluation by speech-language pathologists, which can be subjective and time-consuming. To improve objectivity, AI-based approaches use machine learning and deep learning models such as Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forests, Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), and LSTMs to automatically classify severity levels from speech data. Recent research focuses on improving model generalization through feature fusion, data augmentation, and speaker normalization techniques. Hybrid frameworks that combine handcrafted acoustic features with deep learning representations have shown promise in enhancing robustness and interpretability. Future work emphasizes the need for larger, more diverse datasets and clinically explainable AI systems to support reliable real-world deployment in dysarthria severity assessment.
0288	Heading3	These systems primarily use acoustic features like pitch, jitter, shimmer, harmonics-to-noise ratio, speech rate, MFCCs, and Mel spectrograms, with some studies incorporating image, text, or multimodal features. Datasets such as TORGO and UA Speech are commonly used for training and evaluation, often achieving high accuracy. Although deep learning models provide strong performance, challenges remain in handling mild severity cases, limited datasets, inter-speaker variability, and high computational costs.
0297	Heading3	Automatic Detection of Articulatory-Based Disfluencies in Primary Progressive Aphasia [8]
0298	Heading3	Primary Progressive Aphasia (PPA) is a neurodegenerative disorder that primarily affects language abilities, often causing articulatory-based disfluencies such as repetitions, distortions, and prolongations. To improve early and objective detection, automated systems analyze recorded speech using temporal features (speech rate, pause duration), spectral features (sound quality and clarity), and articulatory features (speech organ movement patterns). These features are extracted from both structured and spontaneous speech samples to capture a wide range of speaking behaviors. Longitudinal speech analysis enables tracking of disease progression over time, supporting personalized treatment planning. Such automated approaches can complement clinical evaluations by providing objective biomarkers for early diagnosis and ongoing monitoring of Primary Progressive Aphasia.
0299	Heading3	Machine learning models such as Support Vector Machines (SVM) and neural networks are used to classify speech segments as fluent or disfluent. Multimodal feature integration, calibration techniques, and transfer learning further enhance accuracy and generalization. By combining acoustic signal processing with AI-based classification, the system provides a consistent, scalable, and clinically useful tool for early PPA detection and monitoring.
0308	Heading3	Automatic Speech and Voice Disorder Detection Using Deep Learning [9]
0309	Heading3	Deep learning has significantly improved the automatic detection of speech and voice disorders such as dysarthria, stuttering, dysphonia, and aphasia. Instead of manual clinical assessment, modern systems use acoustic feature extraction techniques like MFCCs, cochleagrams, Hilbert spectra, and time-frequency analysis to convert raw speech into meaningful data for classification.
0310	Heading3	Advanced neural network models including CNNs, DNNs, LSTMs, GRUs, and self-supervised models like wav2vec 2.0 are widely used for accurate detection. These models effectively capture spectral and temporal speech patterns, achieving high accuracy, though challenges such as limited datasets and lack of standard evaluation methods still remain. Recent studies explore multimodal learning and transfer learning to mitigate data scarcity and improve cross-dataset robustness. Future research focuses on explainable deep learning models to enhance clinical trust and facilitate real-world adoption of automated speech disorder detection systems. Additionally, real-time deployment of these models enables continuous monitoring and early intervention in clinical and telehealth applications. Integrating domain knowledge from speech pathology with deep learning architectures further improves model reliability and interpretability.
0319	Heading3	Augmenting Automatic Speech Recognition Models With Disfluency Detection [10]
0320	Heading3	The proposed approach enhances Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems to better detect disfluencies in spontaneous speech without retraining the core ASR model. It uses an inference-time augmentation pipeline combining Wav2Vec2 feature extraction, a modified Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) forced alignment, and a gap-based classifier. The modified CTC alignment introduces controlled space-token probabilities to create meaningful gaps where disfluent speech may occur. A classifier built on top of Wav2Vec2 then labels each gap as either silence or disfluent speech, achieving around 81% accuracy and improving coverage of previously untranscribed words.
0321	Heading3	This inference-only framework preserves original transcription quality while capturing disfluency events, making it practical for real-time deployment. The system supports applications in speech disorder analysis (e.g., stuttering or aphasia), conversational AI, and adaptive speech feedback tools. By integrating CTC alignment techniques and self-supervised models like Wav2Vec2, the method improves ASR robustness in real-world conversational settings without modifying the base recognition architecture.

0360	Heading2	PROPOSED SYSTEM
0361	Heading2	The proposed system, SpeechWell, is an intelligent speech analysis platform designed to automatically evaluate speech fluency and identify potential speech disorders using audio processing, machine learning, and rule-based techniques. The system accepts speech input from users, extracts relevant acoustic and linguistic features, performs disorder analysis, and provides detailed reports along with personalized exercise recommendations. The entire system is organized into three well-defined modules to ensure modularity, scalability, and ease of maintenance.
0362	Heading2	Unlike conventional clinical methods that rely on subjective evaluation by speech-language pathologists, SpeechWell applies a data-driven approach to ensure consistency and efficiency. Traditional assessments can be time-consuming and may vary between evaluators, whereas the proposed system standardizes the evaluation process through computational analysis. This makes the platform particularly useful for individuals who may not have easy access to professional therapy services.
0363	Heading2	The first module serves as the input and preprocessing stage of the proposed system. In this module, users can either upload a recorded speech sample or record live speech using a microphone interface. The collected audio is first stored in the backend for processing. To maintain consistency across different audio inputs, the system converts all audio files into a mono channel and resamples them to a standard sampling rate of 16 kHz using FFmpeg. This normalization step ensures that subsequent processing and analysis are reliable and independent of input format variations.
0364	Heading2	After normalization, the speech signal is segmented into timestamped speech segments. Each segment represents a continuous portion of speech with defined start and end times. Using these timestamps, the system computes important temporal features such as segment duration, total speech duration, and pause durations between consecutive speech segments. In addition, the system generates a speech transcript from which the total number of spoken words is extracted. Using the total speech duration and word count, the speaking rate is calculated. Furthermore, statistical pause features such as average pause duration and maximum pause duration are computed. These extracted features collectively capture the fluency characteristics of the speaker and are passed to the next module for disorder analysis.
0365	Heading2	The next module is responsible for analyzing the extracted features and identifying potential speech disorders. This module uses a hybrid approach that combines machine learning models with rule-based logic to improve robustness and interpretability. The fluency features and acoustic embeddings obtained from Module 1 are first prepared and scaled before being processed by the classification models.
0366	Heading2	For Dysarthria detection, a supervised machine learning model based on logistic regression is used. The model computes a probability score indicating the likelihood of dysarthria, which is then converted into a class label using a predefined threshold. In parallel, rule-based techniques are applied to detect stuttering by identifying repetitions, prolongations, and abnormal pauses. Grammar analysis is performed by comparing the original transcript with a corrected version, allowing the system to estimate grammar error probability. Phonological errors are detected using pronunciation rules and substitution checks. All these analyses are combined into a unified decision payload containing disorder probabilities and detailed observations.
0367	Heading2	The use of both statistical models and rule-based methods ensures that the system remains interpretable while achieving good classification performance, as reflected in the validation results obtained during experimentation.
0368	Heading2	The third module focuses on result management, user feedback, and continuous improvement support. The disorder probabilities and analysis results generated by Module 2 are stored in a database for future reference and progress tracking. This module computes severity levels for each detected issue and calculates an overall speech score to summarize the user’s performance.
0369	Heading2	A key component of this module is the exercise mapping engine, which maps detected speech issues to appropriate therapy exercises using rule-based logic. Based on the severity of dysarthria, stuttering, grammar, and phonological errors, the system recommends targeted speech exercises and prioritizes them according to the severity level. This personalized recommendation helps users focus on areas that require the most improvement.
0370	Heading2	Additionally, it generates a comprehensive PDF report containing speech metrics, disorder probabilities, severity levels, and recommended exercises. These reports are stored in the system and can be downloaded by the user. The frontend dashboard visualizes progress trends and historical data, allowing users to monitor improvement over time and access training resources through the Therapy Hub.
0371	Heading2	The overall architecture of the SpeechWell system is designed to ensure seamless interaction between the modules while maintaining efficiency and scalability. Each module operates independently but communicates through well-defined data interfaces, allowing the system to process speech data in a structured pipeline. This modular design not only simplifies system development and debugging but also allows future enhancements, such as integrating more advanced machine learning models or expanding the range of detectable speech disorders, without affecting the entire system.
0372	Heading2	In addition to its technical capabilities, the SpeechWell system is designed with accessibility and usability in mind. The user-friendly frontend interface allows individuals with minimal technical knowledge to easily record, upload, and analyze their speech. By combining automated analysis with personalized feedback and progress tracking, the system empowers users to actively participate in their speech improvement journey. This makes SpeechWell a practical and scalable solution for supporting speech therapy, especially in scenarios where professional resources are limited or unavailable.

0386	Heading3	Feature Extraction Module
0387	Heading3	The Feature Extraction module is the first stage of the SpeechWell system and is responsible for converting raw audio input into meaningful numerical representations. The process begins with audio capture, where the user provides speech input either through recording or uploading a file. This raw audio is then passed through a preprocessing stage, where noise removal, normalization and silence trimming are performed. These steps ensure that the audio signal is clean and consistent, reducing the impact of environmental noise and recording variations on subsequent analysis.
0388	Heading3	Following preprocessing, the system performs Voice Activity Detection (VAD) to identify segments of the audio that contain actual speech. This allows the system to eliminate non-speech portions and focus only on relevant speech data. The detected speech segments are then processed using framing and windowing techniques, which divide the continuous signal into smaller frames for detailed analysis. This step is crucial for capturing temporal variations in speech and preparing the signal for feature extraction.
0389	Heading3	The processed frames are then passed through the Wav2Vec2 model to extract acoustic embeddings, which capture deep speech characteristics such as tone, articulation, and pronunciation patterns. In parallel, fluency metrics such as speech duration, pause duration, speaking rate and pause frequency are calculated. These extracted features are combined to form structured feature data, which serves as the input to the classification module for further analysis.
0390	Heading3	Classification Module
0391	Heading3	The Classification module is responsible for analyzing the extracted features and identifying potential speech disorders. The process begins with grammar correction, where the transcribed speech is analyzed to detect grammatical inconsistencies. This helps in estimating the linguistic quality of the speech. The system then evaluates speech fluency by analyzing patterns such as long or frequent pauses, variations in speech speed (fast or slow speech), and repetition of words or sounds, which are key indicators of speech disorders.
0392	Heading3	Based on these observations, the system assigns pattern indicators that quantify the presence of specific speech irregularities. These indicators are then combined with acoustic and fluency features and normalized to ensure consistency in scale. This normalization step is essential for improving the performance of the classification model, as it allows all features to contribute effectively without bias toward larger numerical values.
0393	Heading3	The normalized features are then passed to a Logistic Regression classifier, which predicts whether the speech is fluent or disordered. The model outputs a probability score that represents the likelihood of a speech disorder. Based on this prediction, the system classifies the speech into categories such as fluent or disordered speech. The classification results are then forwarded to the next module for generating recommendations and tracking user progress.

0419	Heading2	IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
0420	Heading2	The implementation of the SpeechWell system follows a modular architecture, where each component is developed independently and integrated through a well-defined workflow. The system is built using a combination of frontend, backend, and machine learning components. The frontend interface enables users to record or upload speech samples, while the backend handles audio processing, feature extraction, and analysis. Communication between the frontend and backend is achieved through RESTful APIs, ensuring smooth data transfer and scalability. The backend is responsible for orchestrating all modules, including preprocessing, feature extraction, classification, and report generation.
0421	Heading2	In the preprocessing stage, audio inputs are standardized to ensure consistent analysis across different devices and formats. The uploaded or recorded audio is converted into a mono channel and resampled to a fixed sampling rate of 16 kHz using FFmpeg. This normalized audio is then segmented into timestamped speech segments. Temporal features such as segment duration, total speech duration, pause duration, and speaking rate are computed. Additionally, speech-to-text conversion is performed to obtain transcripts, which are used for linguistic analysis. These preprocessing steps ensure that both acoustic and textual information are available for further processing.
0422	Heading2	The feature extraction and analysis module integrates both machine learning and rule-based techniques. Acoustic features such as embeddings and MFCC representations are extracted to capture speech characteristics like pitch, tone, and articulation. These features, along with fluency metrics, are scaled and passed to the classification model. A Logistic Regression model is implemented for dysarthria detection, where probability scores are generated and thresholded to produce final predictions. Simultaneously, rule-based methods are used to detect stuttering, grammar errors, and phonological inconsistencies. This hybrid approach improves interpretability and ensures that both learned patterns and explicit speech irregularities are considered during analysis.
0423	Heading2	The final implementation stage focuses on result management, user feedback, and progress tracking. The classification outputs and analysis results are stored in a database, enabling users to track their performance over time. A decision tree-based module is used to recommend personalized speech therapy exercises based on the detected disorders and their severity levels. The system also generates detailed PDF reports that include speech metrics, disorder probabilities, and recommended exercises. The frontend dashboard visualizes user progress through graphs and historical data, providing continuous feedback and encouraging consistent practice. This integrated implementation ensures that the system not only performs accurate analysis but also supports long-term speech improvement.
